01 — Taxis

Show the driver. Get there.

Tap a stop and turn the phone round: its name and address fill the screen in Chinese, under 请带我去, "please take me to". No spelling out pinyin, no pointing at a map the driver can't read.

  • Chinese names and addresses on every stop
  • Copy them in a tap, for a ride app or a message

02 — Maps

Pins where the place really is.

Maps in mainland China use a shifted grid, so a place from Google can land a street or two away. Pawsport corrects every pin, places small shops at their building from the Chinese address, and takes an Amap link as the exact spot.

  • Paste an Amap link: the stop takes that place
  • Metro directions open in Amap

03 — Closing days

The Forbidden City is shut on Mondays.

Many of China's big sights close one day a week, and plenty need a ticket booked days ahead with your passport. Pawsport knows which, flags a stop planned on its closing day, and reminds you when tickets go on sale.

Why are map pins wrong in China?

Maps in mainland China use a shifted coordinate system, so places from Google can land hundreds of metres from where they really are. Pawsport corrects every one before it goes on your map.

Can I paste an Amap link?

Yes. Paste an Amap share link into a stop and Pawsport takes that exact place, with its Chinese name and address.

What does the taxi card show?

The place's name and full address in Chinese, large enough to read across a taxi, under 请带我去 (please take me to).

What about Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan?

Their maps aren't shifted, so Pawsport leaves their pins as they are.
